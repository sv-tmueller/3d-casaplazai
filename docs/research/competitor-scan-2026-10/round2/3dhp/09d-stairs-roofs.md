## 1. Summary

- **Roofs are parametric, not drawn.** A single builder, `HP3D.buildRoof({footprint|points, style, pitch, overhang, thickness, baseY, wallHeight, level, meta})`, produces every roof. Style is one of **`flat`, `shed`, `hip`, `gable`**; `pitch` is **clamped to 0–70°** (default **30°**), `overhang` **floored at 0 m** (default **0.3 m**), flat-slab `thickness` default **0.2 m** (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor` `buildRoof` @ plan region 9464–9959; `hp-sync` overlay toggle).
- **Hip and gable are computed by a real straight skeleton, not by a bounding box.** The builder loads `assets/js/vendor/straight-skeleton.js` lazily, feeds it the (overhang-offset) outline plus holes, and turns each skeleton face into a plane whose height is `baseY + skeletonHeight × tan(pitch)` (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
- **Three geometric paths, one of which makes gables possible.** The general path builds the roof over the raw straight skeleton; a second, precise path looks for a skeleton face standing over a single wall edge whose apex is shared by exactly three faces, and **snaps that apex onto the wall line** (only when the projection lands 5 %–95 % along the edge) — degenerating the end slope into a flat triangular gable instead of a hipped tent; a third, degenerate path handles a plan whose vertices are all on one circle by fanning a single apex (pyramid/cone), used for round and regular rooms (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
- **"Roof follows the walls" is a per-level auto-roof.** A checkbox in the 2D-planner overlay (`#autoRoofToggle`) with a pitch spinner (`#autoRoofPitch`, min 5 / max 60 / default 30) stores one spec per level — `{style:'hip', pitch, overhang:0.3}` — in the project state; whenever the 2D plan's walls for that level change, the previous roof mesh (tagged `src:'sync-roof'`, `locked`) is disposed and rebuilt from the wall endpoints (`OBSERVED`, `hp-sync`).
- **Stairs are never modelled.** They are ordinary catalog objects recognised by name/IFC class. A one-shot *fit* pass measures the object, computes the storey-above base (`levelBase(n+1)+0.06` or the slab's top), **scales the object on Y to the exact gap** and re-seats it; a separate pass then **boolean-subtracts an extruded copy of the stair footprint from the slab above with Babylon CSG**, saving the original vertex data so the hole can be restored (`OBSERVED`, `hp-stairs`).

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 The parametric roof builder (`HP3D.buildRoof`)

**Signature and options** (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`; the option property names are the ones the builder reads).

| Option | Meaning | Default / clamp |
|---|---|---|
| `footprint` | `{outer:[{x,z}…], holes:[[…]]}` explicit outline | — |
| `points` | shorthand: an `outer` ring only | — |
| (neither) | outline is **derived from the level's walls** (see 2.3) | — |
| `style` | `'flat' \| 'shed' \| 'hip' \| 'gable'` (case-insensitive) | `'hip'` |
| `pitch` | degrees from horizontal | `30`, **clamped 0–70°** |
| `overhang` | metres the roof plane projects past the wall line | `0.3`, **≥ 0** |
| `thickness` | flat-roof slab thickness | `0.2` |
| `baseY` | eave/plate height (world Y) | `levelBase(level) + (wallHeight ?? projectWallHeight)` |
| `wallHeight` | override for the eave height | project's wall height |
| `level` | storey index the roof is registered on | current level |
| `meta` | extra metadata merged into the mesh | — |

- **Overhang is applied as a polygon offset, not as a modified slope.** For a positive overhang the outer ring is offset **outward** and each hole ring **inward**, then the result is re-wound; the vertex heights are `plateY + skeletonHeight × tan(pitch)` computed at the *skeleton's* coordinates, i.e. the plane is the same, it just extends one offset further (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`). `INFERRED`: an offset polygon rather than a "roof above a wall with a separate soffit" keeps the eave line parallel to the wall, which is the simple, expected result for a rectangular plan.
- **Flat and near-flat styles short-circuit.** `style === 'flat'` or `pitch < 1°` produces a **slab**: earcut triangulation of the outline at `baseY`, the same ring again at `baseY + thickness`, and vertical side walls around the ring and around every hole, welded by a "keep the edge if it is strictly between the two heights" test, with front-face detection for the winding (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
- **Shed** = a single linear plane fitted to the outline: the builder computes a local frame — a **minimum-area bounding rectangle over the hull** (a rotating-calipers pass over each hull edge, keeping the smallest axis-aligned box area, then swapping so `length` is the long side) — takes the signed perpendicular distance `d` of each vertex from that rectangle's centreline, and lifts each vertex by `plateY + (d + width/2) × tan(pitch)`, i.e. the low eave is one long side of the fitted rectangle and the high eave the other. It then builds the sloped cap, the vertical fascia (skipping edges whose two ends are already at the plate), and an earcut floor cap (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
- **Hip and gable** take the skeleton route in 2.2.
- **A third, special path: the "pyramid/tent" hip.** If the outline has **no holes** and *every* vertex of it lies on the **same** circle (i.e. the plan is a regular polygon or a circle), the builder skips the skeleton entirely: it puts a single apex at `(cx, plateY + R × tan(pitch), cz)` and fans triangles to each outline vertex, tagged `style:'hip'` (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`). `INFERRED`: this is why a round or square room gets a clean conical/pyramidal roof rather than the degenerate output a skeleton would give for a perfectly symmetric plan.
- **Roof materials/UVs.** The top surface is two-sided-lit; if a `texture` was passed it is applied with `uvScale 0.5`, else the diffuse falls back to a brown `0.55,0.32,0.24`. The underside/plate mesh is grey `0.25`, gable end triangles are a light `0.93,0.92,0.89` with back-face culling off. A separate exported helper, `HP3D.roofUVs(mesh)`, re-projects a roof mesh's UVs so the texture runs in the slope plane (skipped when the mesh metadata says `uv === 2`), i.e. **UVs are recomputed from geometry rather than authored** (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`; `hp-bim` applies `roofUVs` on import/load when an `autoRoof` lacks them).
- **Result mesh.** The three parts are merged into one mesh, re-centred (the mesh's bounding-box centre is baked out and copied to `position`), then named `autoRoof` (`id` and `name`), made pickable, added as a shadow caster, and given `metadata.hp = {kind:'roof', level, style, pitch, overhang, uv:2, …}` (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
- **Failure modes are explicit strings**, all `OBSERVED` in `hp-editor`: `"No footprint for the roof"`, `"Roof skeleton failed"`, `"Straight skeleton failed to generate."`, `"Earcut not available for roof triangulation."`, `"Unable to load straight skeleton library."`, `"Automatic roof generation failed:"`, `"Multiple disjoint footprints detected. Generating roof for the primary outline only."`, `"Roof could not follow the walls"` (this last one in `hp-sync`).

### 2.2 How hip and gable are actually computed

- **Skeleton.** The outline is converted to a 2-D ring list (outer CCW, holes CW), handed to `SkeletonBuilder.buildFromPolygon([...])`, and the library returns `{vertices:[[x,y,h],…], polygons:[[i,j,k],…]}` where `h` is the skeleton's inward distance (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
- **Height mapping.** Every skeleton vertex becomes `(x + offsetX, plateY + h × tan(pitch), z + offsetZ)`. So **those are real raised ridge/hip lines**, and the faces are triangulated from the skeleton's own faces with a per-face normal. `INFERRED`: this is why a hip roof over a non-rectangular plan gets correct valley/hip lines for free, but the same code path produces **valleys** for an L-shaped plan rather than refusing it.
- **The gable special case.** Before building, the code finds which outline edges are "flat enough" (turn angle `> 0.004 rad` and `< 0.55 rad`, i.e. concave/convex but nearly straight) and which are not; runs of same-sign flat edges are candidates. A candidate is accepted only if all its points are co-circular (a circumcentre is fitted to the first, middle and last vertex of the run) with radius **0.2–300 m** and every run vertex within `max(3 cm, 2 % of R)` of that circle — i.e. **only straight or gently-curved wall runs can be a gable end** (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
- **Ridge snapping.** In the skeleton output, the builder looks for a triangular face whose last index is a *consecutive pair* of original outline vertices (a face standing over one wall edge) and whose apex is a skeleton node shared by exactly three faces. For each such face it projects the apex onto the edge; if the projection parameter is **outside 5 %–95 %** of the edge length, or that ridge node was already claimed, the face is skipped. The apex vertex is then **moved onto that exact point** on the edge — the ridge node is snapped to the wall line (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
- **Precise gable fallback.** If the snapping produced nothing *and* the plan has no curved runs, the builder stops using the skeleton and runs a dedicated routine: take the same minimum-area rectangle frame (centre, length, width, unit vectors), build a **prism** with two sloped planes meeting at a ridge at mid-width running along the long axis, height `plateY + (width/2) × tan(pitch)`, plus the two gable end triangles, and use `style:'gable', pitch, overhang` in the metadata. `INFERRED`: this is the "give the user a gable even when the skeleton is ambiguous or the plan is a plain rectangle" safety net, and it explains why the *Gable roof* tile is described in the UI as a distinct tool rather than a style switch.
- **Main gable path.** Otherwise the code lifts every skeleton vertex by `h × tan(pitch)` and triangulates the skeleton faces; when a face is the snapped ridge face it re-orders the triangle by the sign of a 3-D cross product so the two roof planes meet cleanly. Finally the whole outline (outer + holes) gets an earcut floor cap at `plateY` (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
- **The "Hip roof" and "Gable roof" tiles are hand-dragged prototypes**, not the parametric builder. Both create a small fixed-size custom polyhedron and register per-handle draggable spheres (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`):
  - `#roofButtonPoly` (*Hip roof*) → a `4poly` solid from **6 handles: 4 eave corners at `(±1, 0, ±4)` plus 2 ridge-end handles at `(0, 1, ±1)`**, the same 6 vertices duplicated 0.25 m below to give the solid thickness. So the ridge is a real 2 m segment at mid-length, the four corners slope up to it and the hip ends slope diagonally to the corners: **a 2 m × 8 m rectangle with a hip ridge, initial eave→ridge pitch `atan(1/1) ≈ 45°` (measured over the 2 m width)**. It is placed at `floorHeight × level + 0.1`, with x/z snapped to the grid, and the whole solid is rebuilt (`geometry.applyToMesh`) on every drag end (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
  - `#roofButtonPoly2` (*Gable roof*) → a `polyt3` solid from **6 handles: 4 eave corners at `(±6, 0, ±4)` and 2 ridge ends at `(±6, 3, 0)`**, duplicated 0.05 m below for thickness. The ridge runs the long way and the two ends are **triangular gable walls**, so the initial eave→ridge pitch is `atan(3/4) ≈ 36.9°` (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).
  - During any drag, a live readout is produced by projecting the drag-plane normal against world-up: **`Roof pitch: {deg}°`** (`fx.roofPitch`); for the eave-corner (`4spheredrag`) handles the drag also toasts `↤ / ↦ / ↥ / ↧ RO offset: {metres}` (`fx.roOffset`), i.e. **eave overhang is reported as four directional offsets measured against a reference element's bounding box** (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`). `INFERRED`: so in this hand-drag mode "pitch" and "overhang" are *observed* numbers, not inputs — the user moves the ridge and reads the resulting angle.
- **"Roof edges" is a polygon-side count, not a hip count.** `#sliderRoof` is a range `min 3 / max 20 / value 4 / step 1` labelled *Roof edges* (`tool.roofEdges`), and `#rooftext` is `"{n} edges"` (`tool.edges`). The plain **Roof** tile uses exactly this `n`: it registers `n` draggable sphere handles (the handle count is the slider value), extrudes a 2-part polygonal plate from them, merges it, seats it at `floorHeight × (level + 1)` — i.e. **at the top of the walls** — and gives it an X rotation of 30° (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor` `#roofButton`; `app.php.html` markup; `evidence/s5-9-build-panel.txt` shows `button "Roof edges −"`, value `4`, `slider "Roof edges": "4"`).
- `INFERRED`: so the product actually offers **two roof models** — one parametric (`buildRoof`, reached from the 2-D sync, the AI `create_roof` tool and the whole-house builder) and one manual (the Build-tab tiles, where "pitch" and "overhang" are emergent from gizmo dragging rather than entered numbers). Section 2.2b tabulates all four producers and their differing defaults.

- **The AI assistant is a third caller, and it is where gable/shed/flat become user-reachable.** `hp-ai-chat` exposes a **`create_roof`** tool whose argument object is `{style, pitch, overhang, level, color, texture, points?:[[x,z],…]}`; it maps `points` into `{x,z}` objects and calls `HP3D.buildRoof(...)` directly, so **the model chooses the style string** (`hip`/`gable`/`shed`/`flat`) with no client-side enum. The same module's whole-house builder, when the plan carries a `roof` block with `style` and `style !== 'none'`, derives the footprint with `footprintOf(roomBounds)` — i.e. from the **rectangular bounds of the AI's own rooms**, not from the drawn walls — then calls `buildRoof({style, pitch: pitch ?? 30, overhang: overhang ?? 0.4, level, footprint, wallHeight, silent:true, texture, color})` (`OBSERVED`, `hp-ai-chat`, locally de-obfuscated in this job). `INFERRED`: note the **third** overhang default — the AI house builder uses **0.4 m** while the 2-D sync uses **0.3 m** and `buildRoof` itself uses 0.3; another symptom of roof parameters being duplicated rather than centralised.

### 2.3 Where the roof outline comes from ("roof follows the walls")

`buildRoof` has three outline sources, tried in order (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`):

1. an explicit `footprint`, or a bare `points` ring;
2. **the level's floor slabs**: it collects `slab` elements on the level that carry `metadata.hp.points`, merges them into `{outer, holes}` and a per-slab `thickness`;
3. **the level's walls**: it walks every `wall` element, takes `wallEndpoints()` (`a`,`b`, `length`, `thickness`, `height`, `baseY`, `curve`), builds end-points with an 8 cm welding tolerance, walks the resulting segment graph into closed rings, and simplifies the outer ring (a convex-hull-style monotone-chain pass).

The slab path is preferred when it is geometrically close to the wall path: the code compares the slab outline with the wall ring and, if every slab vertex is within **0.35 m** of the wall ring, uses the slab union; otherwise it falls back to the wall ring. If the slab-derived outline came from the walls themselves, the roof is offset outward by **half the average wall thickness**. The **curve** case matters: a curved wall is sampled (`arcLength / radius`) into a polyline before it enters the ring walk, so **arc walls are supported by the roof footprint** (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor`).

### 2.4 "Roof follows the walls" — the sync mechanism

- **State.** `HPSync` keeps `roofs` as a map keyed by level index. `getState()`/`setState()` serialise it as `{plans, meshless, roofs, meter}`, so a project file carries, per level, the roof spec — but not the roof mesh (`OBSERVED`, `hp-sync`).
- **UI.** In the 2D-planner overlay the sync module injects, next to the frame container: *Live sync 2D ⇄ 3D* (`#hpSyncToggle`), *Floors follow the rooms* (`#hpSyncSlabs`) and its own checkbox `#hpSyncToggle`'s sibling — the roof control is the pre-existing `#autoRoofToggle`, whose label span the sync module rewrites to **`sync.roof` = "Roof follows the walls"**; its pitch spinner is `#autoRoofPitch` (markup `min 5 max 60 value 30`, `p2d.pitch` = "Pitch (°)"). The checkbox itself is labelled `p2d.genRoof` = **"Generate roof"** (`OBSERVED`, `hp-sync`; markup in `app.php.html` and in the captured live DOM; labels from `app-php-i18n.json`).
- **Behaviour.** Ticking the box sets `roofs[currentLevel] = {style:'hip', pitch: parseFloat(spinner)||30, overhang: 0.3}` and triggers a rebuild; unticking deletes the entry and the roof. Editing the spinner while a spec exists patches `roof.pitch` and rebuilds. A rebuild disposes every `roof` element on that level whose `metadata.hp.src === 'sync-roof'` (silently), then calls `buildRoof({level, silent:true, meta:{src:'sync-roof'}, ...spec})` (`OBSERVED`, `hp-sync`).
- **Triggers.** The rebuild runs after plan diffs that changed the level's walls, after a level change, and on project state restore. Errors are swallowed to `console.warn('Roof could not follow the walls', …)`. `INFERRED`: the roof is regenerated from scratch every time, so **it is always the hip style and always overhang 0.3** in this "auto" mode; gable, shed and flat are reachable only through the parametric builder's explicit options — which in practice means the AI `create_roof` tool (2.2b) — or the drag tiles (2.2), not through this toggle.
- **The clamp is inconsistent between the two paths** (`OBSERVED`): the overlay's spinner is clamped `1°…80°` (`min(max(v,1),0x50)`, fallback 30) while `buildRoof` clamps `0°…70°` (`min(max(v,30),0x46)`). So a pitch typed as 75° in the overlay is silently rebuilt at 70°, and the spinner still shows 75. `INFERRED`: a real (if minor) bug, and a useful reminder to have **one** clamp function shared by the UI and the geometry.
- **2D-planner feedback.** The bridge re-renders each synced element as an SVG-ish group; for `kind === 'roof'` with a 2-point `ridge` it appends a `line.fp-b3d-ridge` between the two ridge points, and roofs carry `locked: metadata.hp.src === 'sync-roof'`. The ridge points come from the `4spheredrag` handles of the 3-D mesh, transformed to plan coordinates (`OBSERVED`, `planner-bridge3d`, `hp-sync`). `INFERRED`: so the 2-D plan shows the roof **outline plus its ridge line**, and it also shows that the synced roof is locked against 2-D editing.

### 2.5 Roof → dimensions, quantities and IFC

- **Roof height is measured, not read from a parameter** (round 1, confirmed here): the dimensions overlay enumerates `kinds:['roof']` and takes the max `worldBounds().max.y` for the overall height dimension, so pitch/overhang/plate height together determine the number on screen (`OBSERVED`, `hp-dims`, round-1 report §2.2).
- `levelOf()` has a **roof-specific rule**: for a `roof` element the storey is `floor((minY - 0.3) + 0.05) / floorHeight`, i.e. roofs are attributed to the storey *below* the plate they sit on, using a 0.3 m tolerance. `INFERRED`: that is exactly the auto-roof overhang default, so a roof generated with the default lands on the level it shelters (`OBSERVED`, `hp-editor` `levelOf`).
- **BIM serialisation.** `hp-bim` writes a `roof` object with `style` (string), plus `pitch` and `overhang` only `if number && isFinite`; the IFC class mapping is `roof → IfcRoof`. The built-in BIM type seed for a roof is named **"Ceramic tile roof"** and its layer stack is **tiles 15 mm / battens 25 mm / rafters 60 mm** (`OBSERVED`, `hp-bim`; `bim.typeSeed.roof`, `bim.mat.roofTile`).
- **Quantities** the roof feeds: `eff.pv.area` *"Usable roof area"* and `eff.rain.area` *"Catchment area (roof)"*, and efficiency checks `eff.rec.roof` *"Roof: U {u} W/m²K above the limit {l}; add insulation…"* with archetypes `eff.a.tileNoCeiling`, `tileCeiling`, `tileSlab`, `flatSlab`, `roofPassive (30 cm)` (`OBSERVED`, `app-php-i18n.json`).
- **Copy semantics**: `copy.group.roofs` copies `kind:'roof'` elements but **excludes the generated `autoRoof` mesh** (round 1), which is consistent with the fact that the auto roof is a regenerated, `sync-roof`-locked artefact while a parametric/user roof is an editable element (`OBSERVED`, `hp-copy`, `hp-sync`).

### 2.6 Stairs — recognition

- There is **no stair element type**. `HPStairs.isStair(mesh)` returns true when, in order (`OBSERVED`, `hp-stairs`):
  1. `metadata.hp.stair === false` → **false** (explicit opt-out);
  2. `metadata.hp.stair === true` **or** `metadata.hp.aiType === 'stairs'` → true;
  3. `metadata.bim.ifcClass` matches `/^IfcStair/i` → true;
  4. the catalog entry for the mesh's model path is looked up: it is a stair if the catalog title/slug matches `/stair|staircase|escada|escalera|escalier|treppe|scala|лестниц|楼梯|階段|계단|ladder|escalator/i`, is **not** `/pool ladder|step ?stool|banqueta/i`, and is either in catalog category `0x39` or `0x98` **or** matches that name regex;
  5. otherwise the `bim.name` / `hp.name` string is matched against the same regex, minus the same exclusion list.
- `INFERRED`: the exclusion list is what stops a pool ladder becoming a staircase — a small but important detail, because the same multilingual vocabulary is reused for outdoor furniture.

### 2.7 Stairs — the fit pass (auto-height)

`HPStairs.fit(mesh)` is a one-shot, guarded by `metadata.hp.stairFitted` (skip if already done) and `metadata.hp.stairFit === false` (skip if opted out) (`OBSERVED`, `hp-stairs`):

1. `root = elementRoot(mesh)`; `bounds = worldBounds(root)`; `H0 = bounds.max.y − bounds.min.y` (current height).
2. `n = levelOf(mesh)`; target top `T = levelBase(n + 1) + 0.06`.
3. **Slab-aware override**: for every `slab` element on level `n+1`, if the stair's centre `(cx,cz)` lies inside the slab's world XY bounds, `T = slab.bounds.max.y`. `INFERRED`: this is what makes a stair land on the finished floor surface rather than on the theoretical storey datum when a slab exists.
4. `gap = T − bounds.min.y`.
5. Mark `stairFitted = true` **before the geometry guards below**, so a rejected stair is not retried every frame.
6. Reject (return, unmodified) if `H0 <= 0.3`, or `gap <= 0.5`, or `ratio = gap/H0` is outside `[0.4, 2.5]`, or `|ratio − 1| < 0.01`. `INFERRED`: the last test means "don't rescale a stair that is already within 1 % of the right height", and the ratio window is a sanity clamp against absurd scale-ups.
7. Otherwise **scale the root on Y by `ratio`** (`scaling.scaleInPlace`), recompute the world matrix, then re-centre: shift X so the object's X centre hits `cx`, shift Z so its Z centre hits `cz`, and shift Y so its bottom sits at the original `bounds.min.y`. Emit `moved {mesh, kind:'object'}`.
- Note that only the **Y scale** changes; the footprint stays as placed, and the object's bottom is preserved (it grows upward) (`OBSERVED`).
- `fit` is applied per object once per session, but a "mark all as fitted" pass (`HPStairs` `_0x1bf7fa`) sets `stairFitted = true` on every stair while a busy dialog is open, so project load does not re-fit stairs that the user has already corrected by hand (`OBSERVED`).

### 2.8 Stairs — the slab hole (boolean subtraction)

The hole pass is separate, idempotent-by-signature, and CSG-based (`OBSERVED`, `hp-stairs`):

1. Collect stair footprints: for every `object` element that `isStair`, take the world bounding box's **four XY corners transformed by the world matrix** and the root's `worldBounds().min.y / max.y`. Additionally, any footprint the 2-D planner reports through `HPSync.stairFootprints()` is merged in.
2. For each `slab` element, compute its outline polygon (`metadata.hp.points` if present, else its world bounding box corners). A stair is a candidate for this slab if `slabTop > stair.y0 + 1` **and** `slabTop < stair.y1 + 0.6` **and** the stair's XY AABB overlaps the slab's. (The `+1` / `+0.6` window is what tolerates the stair sticking up through the slab.)
3. **Signature cache**: a per-slab string built from the slab's transform plus the candidate polygons (rounded to 3 decimals). If unchanged, nothing happens.
4. On change: **restore** the slab's original geometry first (`_hpStairOrig.vd` / `.subs`, saved on the first cut), then for each candidate ring:
   - convert to a `Vector3` ring at `y=0`, reverse if the winding is negative;
   - `MeshBuilder.ExtrudePolygon('stairHole', {shape, depth}, scene, earcut)` where `depth = (bounds.max.y − bounds.min.y) + 1` and the mesh is centred at the slab's mid-height;
   - `csg = csg.subtract(CSG.FromMesh(holeMesh))`, then dispose the hole mesh;
   - `csg.toMesh('stairCut', originalMaterial, scene, true)`, extract its vertex data and remap sub-mesh indices back into range of the original multi-material, then write the carved geometry back onto the original slab mesh.
5. On success the slab's `metadata.hp.holes` is set to the (snapped-to-3-decimals) hole polygons; when there are no candidates, `holes` is deleted. Failures log `"Stair opening failed"` / `"Stair openings failed"`.
- **The hole outline is a shrunk copy of the stair footprint.** Footprints coming from the 3-D side are inflated by a per-vertex offset derived from the local corner angle: the offset magnitude is `0.02 / max(0.3, cos(halfAngle))`, i.e. ≈ 2 cm with an amplification at sharp corners (`OBSERVED`, `hp-stairs`). `INFERRED`: that is a **clearance** so the stair geometry does not z-fight the slab edge, and it is why the hole is slightly larger than the stair.
- **The 2-D planner's own stair footprints** are built from the plan object's `size` and `thick` and `angle` (a rotated rectangle), with `y0 = levelBase(level)` and `y1 = levelBase(level+1)` (`OBSERVED`, `hp-sync` `stairFootprints`). `INFERRED`: so a stair drawn as a 2-D block still cuts the floor above even if no 3-D catalog model was loaded.
- **Re-run policy**: `HPStairs.update` runs on `projectLoaded`/`imported` immediately, on a **60 ms debounce** for every other element event except `material`/`contours`/`renderMode`, *and* on a **700 ms poll** while the document is visible (`OBSERVED`, `hp-stairs`). `INFERRED`: the poll is a belt-and-braces catch for edits that do not emit events.
- `INFERRED` (limitations, worth recording because they shape CasaPlazAI's design): the hole is a CSG boolean kept only in memory; there is no riser/tread/headroom model, no "stair must land on a slab" validation, no automatic re-fit after the user manually resizes a stair (`stairFitted` latches), and the stair hole is cut **per stair footprint** so two overlapping stairs would cut two holes.

### 2.9 2-D planner side (context for the above)

- The 2-D planner exposes a *Stairs* tool (`tool.stairs` = "Stairs", hint "Add a staircase", message `msg.addStairs` = "Add staircase") which instantiates a `floorplanEditor.obj2D('free', 'stair', 'simpleStair', …)` object; the symbol library carries `straight-stairs` and `spiral-stairs` glyphs, and the catalog exposes `itemGroup.stairs` "Stairways", `item.straight-stairs` "Stairs (3D model)", `item.spiral-stairs` "Spiral stairs" (`OBSERVED`, `planner-planner-main`, `planner-symbols`; `2dplanner-i18n.json`; the captured 2-D planner page `evidence/2D-Planner-index.php.html` contains `tool.stairs`, `msg.addStairs` and a `stair_mode` control).

## 3. Ideas for CasaPlazAI

CasaPlazAI today (from `~/dsh-workspaces/rea2-shared/casaplazai-schema.md`): `FloorplanData` v1 with `wallHeight`, `wallThickness`, `rooms[]` (CCW polygons, `wallsOverride[]`, optional per-segment `roofs[{segmentIndex, leanDeg}]`, `0 ≤ leanDeg ≤ 75`), `openings[]`, `furniture[]` (7 types). Roofs are **per-walled-segment lean angles with a derived ridge**, permitted only on the topmost floor; `roof-unsupported-config` explicitly rules out 3+ leans ("valleys/hips out of scope") and `roof-invalid-gable` requires the opposing pair to be parallel, equal-length, aligned and facing. There is no stair entity and no floor-slab entity. Ranked by value ÷ effort; each idea names the finding it comes from.

### Tier 1 — high value, low effort

1. **Ship a "flat / shed" roof pair first, and make the shipped lean the shed case.** *(findings 2.1, 2.4, 2.5)*
   The competitor's `flat` and `shed` paths are the cheap ones: flat is earcut at `baseY` plus the same ring at `baseY + thickness` plus a ring wall; shed is a single linear lift `plateY + (d + width/2)·tan(pitch)` from the outline's principal axis. CasaPlazAI's schema already has per-segment `leanDeg` on the topmost floor and derives the ridge at compile time, so **a flat roof is expressible today as all-zero leans** and a shed is one positive lean. What is missing is (a) a `roofStyle`/global pitch on the plan rather than asking for a lean per wall, and (b) the *renderer* half — an earcut cap plus a fascia ribbon around the plate. Concretely: add an optional plan-level `roof: {kind:'flat'|'shed'|'gable'|'hip', pitchDeg, overhang, thickness}` whose shed/gable cases **compile down into the existing `roofs[{segmentIndex, leanDeg}]` rows**, so the frozen schema does not change for the common cases. This buys a real roof in the 3D view for very little code and no schema churn.

2. **Adopt the competitor's numeric contract for pitch, overhang and plate height.** *(finding 2.1)*
   Three constants, copied because they are well-chosen and cost nothing: **pitch default 30°, builder clamp 0–70°** (and note the *inconsistency*: the auto-roof spinner clamps a *looser* `max(max(v,1),80)` — 1–80° — while the builder clamps 0–70°, so the value a user types in the overlay can be silently reduced when the roof is rebuilt; pick one range), **overhang default 0.3 m, never negative**, **flat-slab thickness 0.2 m**. Expose the *UI* range as **5–60° in 1° steps** (the competitor's spinner markup), because that is the range a user can meaningfully judge, and keep 0–70 as the validator clamp. In CasaPlazAI terms: one `roof` block on the plan, three fields, plus validator codes `roof-pitch-out-of-range` and `roof-negative-overhang` alongside the existing `bad-lean`/`roof-*` family. The pitch must be **degrees from horizontal** (not a slope ratio) — matching both the competitor and the existing `leanDeg`.

3. **"Roof follows the walls" as a per-floor toggle, stored as one spec per floor.** *(findings 2.3, 2.4)*
   This is the feature users actually notice, and CasaPlazAI can do it more cheaply than the competitor because it already has a room/wall model: a floor-level `autoRoof: {style:'gable', pitchDeg, overhang} | null` in the *editor-local* state (like `casaplaza.snap.settings.v1`), a single "Roof follows the walls" checkbox in the inspector, and a rebuild on any wall edit for that floor. Derive the outline by unioning the floor's room polygons (the competitor unions slabs first, then falls back to wall endpoints, then offsets outward by half the wall thickness — a simpler equivalent for CasaPlazAI is "offset the room union outward by `overhang`"). Keep the competitor's honest failure messages as toasts: *"no footprint for the roof"*, *"roof needs at least three walls"*, *"roof could not follow the walls"*.

4. **Recompute roof UVs from geometry instead of authoring them.** *(finding 2.1)*
   `HP3D.roofUVs(mesh)` re-projects the roof mesh's UVs by slope direction, and the builder tags every generated roof with `uv:2` so the expensive pass runs once. In a Three.js viewer this is ~20 lines (`uv = (dot(p, u), dot(p, v))` per vertex using the slope axes) and it is the difference between a texture that slides across the roof and one that reads as tiles. Low effort, visible payoff.

### Tier 2 — high value, medium effort

5. **Extend `roofs` to a real hip via a straight-skeleton or a hip-by-inset, gated behind a validator.** *(findings 2.1, 2.2)*
   The competitor's hip path is a straight skeleton (it ships `straight-skeleton.js` and calls `buildFromPolygon`). Building a skeleton is a large piece of work, so for CasaPlazAI I would **not** copy that. Instead: for a *rectangular* room union, a hip roof is `gable ∪ two end triangles`, and for an L-shape a "hip by uniform inset" (offset the outline inward by `rise/tan(pitch)`, that inner ring is the ridge plateau) is a few dozen lines and degrades gracefully. Gate it behind the existing `roof-unsupported-config` code and rename the error for 3+ leans to something like `roof-hip-not-supported` so the schema tells the truth. This also directly satisfies the current `roof-unsupported-config` ("3+ positive leans — valleys/hips out of scope"): keep that rule, and add hips as a *plan-level* style that compiles into at most two lean rows on rectangular rooms.

6. **Two-click gable: let the user pick the two gable-end walls, then derive the ridge.** *(findings 2.2, 2.4)*
   The competitor spends an enormous amount of code (straight skeleton, co-circular edge detection, ridge snapping with the 5 %/95 % rule, a prism fallback) to *guess* which pairs of walls are the gable ends. CasaPlazAI's schema already requires a valid opposing pair for a gable (`roof-invalid-gable`, `roof-length-mismatch`), so **ask instead of guessing**: when the user turns the gable on, highlight the topmost-floor walls and let them click the two ends. That converts a hard geometry problem into a 20-line selection UI, and it makes the validator's error codes unreachable rather than user-visible. Show the derived ridge as a dashed line in Plan mode, mirroring the competitor's `fp-b3d-ridge` in its 2-D plan.

7. **Stair object that auto-fits and cuts the floor above.** *(findings 2.7, 2.8)*
   CasaPlazAI has no slab entity, so the hole has to be expressed differently — and that is an opportunity to do it *better*. Two options, in order of preference:
   - **(a) Hole as editor-local geometry, not schema.** Keep a per-floor list of `{polygon, levelId}` stair openings in the editor-local state (like the underlay key), and have the 3D viewer's floor mesh subtract them (Three.js `Shape` + `holes` is built for exactly this, so no CSG library is needed). Copy the competitor's **numeric policy** verbatim in spirit: hole ring = stair footprint **inflated ~2 cm**, top = the floor slab's top, and the stair scaled so its bottom stays put and its top reaches the floor above **plus a 6 cm tolerance**.
   - **(b) Schema-level, only if (a) proves insufficient.** The frozen v1 has no room-holes concept, so this would need `Room.innerRings?: [x,y][][]` plus a `migrate.ts` step to `schemaVersion: 2`. Do not do this first; the underlay precedent (`casaplaza.underlay.v1`) shows editor-local state is the sanctioned place for per-floor trace-like data.
   Either way, adopt the competitor's **one-shot latch** (`stairFitted`) so a stair is not rescaled on every render, and its **restore-original-then-recut** pattern so re-running the cut is idempotent.

8. **Refuse-to-guess guards around stairs, learned from their gaps.** *(findings 2.7, 2.9)*
   The competitor's `fit` silently gives up in several situations (gap ≤ 0.5 m, ratio outside 0.4–2.5, ratio within 1 % of 1) and latches a flag that permanently stops retries. For CasaPlazAI, surface each of those as a non-blocking inspector note instead: *"this stair is 12 % off the storey height — scale it?"*, *"no floor above this stair"*, *"floor-to-floor is 0.4 m here; check the level heights"*. All of the inputs already exist (`wallHeight`, floor list, element bounds). This is the same idea as round 1's "norm/consistency warnings" but specifically for stairs, and it turns their silent failure into the app's judgement.

### Tier 3 — valuable but larger

9. **Stair parameters in the schema, and implement the norm row they declared but never wrote.** *(findings 2.8, 2.9)*
   The competitor hard-codes `norms.stair` = *"Stairs (max riser / min tread / min width)"* in its translation map with no code behind it, and its stairs are just scaled catalog meshes. If CasaPlazAI adds stairs, a small `Stair` entity with `risers`, `treadDepth`, `width`, `directionDeg`, `levelId` is more honest than a scaled model, and it makes three checks trivial: riser ≤ ~0.2 m, tread ≥ ~0.25 m, width ≥ ~0.8 m, plus a headroom check against the floor above. `INFERRED`: this is the single place where the competitor's shortcut is visible in the product, so it is the best place to be visibly better.

10. **Roof → quantities, and roof attribution to the floor below.** *(finding 2.5)*
    Bake the roof's slope area, ridge length and eave length as derived quantities for a schedule/CSV, and attribute a roof to the storey *below* its plate using the competitor's `minY − 0.3` rule — with `0.3` being the default overhang, that rule is exactly "a roof belongs to the floor it shelters", and copying it costs one line while avoiding an off-by-one-floor bug in every schedule.

### Anti-patterns worth avoiding (learned from the competitor)

- **Do not bolt a hand-dragged prototype roof next to a parametric one.** The competitor ships both: the *Roof / Hip roof / Gable roof* tiles build small **fixed-size** polyhedra (a 2 m × 8 m ridge, a 12 m × 8 m gable) whose pitch you get by dragging gizmo spheres, while the 2-D overlay's *Generate roof* builds a fully parametric hip from the plan. Two mental models for one noun — and the three tiles don't even agree on where a roof goes: the plain *Roof* plate is seated at `floorHeight × (level + 1)` (top of the storey's walls), while *Hip roof* and *Gable roof* are seated at `floorHeight × level + 0.1` (the floor). CasaPlazAI should have **one** roof concept (ideas 5/6) and let numeric fields drive it.
- **Do not call a polygon-side-count a roof parameter.** "Roof edges 3–20" is the number of sides of the roof *plate*, sitting next to "Floor edges 3–20" for the floor plate. Read as a roof control it is meaningless. Name it honestly (`Roof shape: N sides`), or drop it and derive the polygon from the rooms.
- **Do not keep valley geometry as an accident.** Because the hip/gable path feeds an arbitrary outline to a straight skeleton, an L-shaped plan silently gets valleys — geometry the schema and the quantity take-off do not describe. If CasaPlazAI supports hips, it should either compute valleys deliberately or refuse the plan shape.
- **Do not put a 700 ms polling loop behind a geometry pass that boolean-subtracts meshes.** That is how the competitor keeps stair holes in sync; it is also why every stair edit re-runs CSG. Event-driven invalidation with a signature check (which they *also* have, and which is the good half) is the part to copy.
